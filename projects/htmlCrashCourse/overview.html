Overview: HTML and the project

HTML (HyperText Markup Language) is not a programming language — there's no logic, no variables, no control flow. It's a markup language: a way of describing the structure and meaning of content, so a browser knows "this is a heading," "this is a list," "this is a link to somewhere else," and can render and behave accordingly.

That distinction matters more than it sounds like it should. A page built with the right structural tags is usable by a screen reader, indexable correctly by search engines, and easy for another developer (or you, in six months) to understand at a glance. A page built entirely out of generic containers with no semantic meaning technically "works" in a browser but throws all of that away.

The project

Across this guide you'll build a single page: a personal developer profile. Think of it as the content a portfolio site or a resume page would have, before any visual design is applied. By the end it will include:

  • An introduction with your name and a short bio
  • A list of skills
  • Links to your projects, GitHub, or social profiles, plus a profile image
  • A table of your experience or education
  • A contact form
  • All of it organized with proper HTML5 semantic layout (header, nav, main, footer)

Each chapter from here on ends with an "Apply it" section — a small addition to make to your actual project file, using only what that chapter just covered. By the time you reach the project checklist near the end, most of the work will already be done in pieces; that chapter is where you step back and look at the whole thing together.
