Text and headings

Headings: h1 through h6

<h1>Page title</h1>
<h2>A major section</h2>
<h3>A subsection</h3>

Headings aren't just "big text" — they define the outline of your document. A screen reader user commonly navigates a page by jumping between headings, the way you'd skim a table of contents. That only works if headings are used to mark actual structure and nest in order:

  • Use exactly one <h1> per page — it's the page's main title.
  • Don't skip levels going down (an <h2> followed directly by an <h4>, with no <h3>, breaks the outline).
  • Never choose a heading level because of how big or bold it happens to look — that's a styling concern, and it isn't one you have yet without CSS. Choose it because of what it structurally is.

Paragraphs

<p>This is a paragraph of text.</p>

Browsers add space before and after a <p> automatically. Don't use empty paragraphs or <br> tags to fake that spacing — use an actual new paragraph, or accept the default spacing until you learn CSS.

Inline text semantics

<p>This is <strong>important</strong> and this is <em>emphasized</em>.</p>

<strong> means the content carries real importance (and browsers happen to render it bold, but that's a side effect, not the purpose). <em> means stress emphasis, rendered italic by default for the same reason. There are older tags, <b> and <i>, that produce the same visual result with no semantic meaning attached — they exist for cases where you want the visual effect specifically with no implication of importance (a book title, for instance). Prefer <strong>/<em> when you actually mean "this matters."

Line breaks and horizontal rules

<p>First line<br>Second line, same paragraph</p>
<hr>

<br> forces a line break within the same block of text (an address, a poem line) — not a substitute for starting a new paragraph. <hr> marks a thematic break between sections of content, not just "a horizontal line I want to see."

Apply it

In profile.html's <body>, add an <h1> with your name, and a short <p> bio underneath it (two or three sentences). Use <strong> around one word or phrase you genuinely want to stand out as important (your current role, for example) — not just to see the bold text.
