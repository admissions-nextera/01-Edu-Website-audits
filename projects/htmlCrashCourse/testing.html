Testing and validating your page

There's no compiler for HTML — a browser will do its best to render even badly broken markup, silently guessing at what you meant. That makes it easy to ship a page with real structural problems that never throw an error anywhere. A few concrete ways to actually check your work:

The W3C Markup Validator

Upload your file (or paste the HTML directly) to the W3C's validator. It checks for real spec violations: unclosed tags, invalid nesting (a <div> inside a <p>, for instance, which isn't allowed), duplicate id values, missing required attributes. Run your finished page through it and fix everything it flags — a clean validation pass is a real, objective signal that your markup is correct, not just "looks right in one browser."

Keyboard-only navigation

Close your mouse hand for a minute. Using only Tab, Shift+Tab, and Enter, try to: reach every nav link, reach and fill out every form field, and submit the form. If you get stuck anywhere, or the visible focus jumps somewhere confusing, that's a real usability problem — not a theoretical accessibility concern, an actual one you just experienced yourself.

Screen reader landmarks (optional but worth trying once)

Turn on your OS's built-in screen reader (VoiceOver on macOS, Narrator on Windows) for a few minutes and navigate your page by headings and landmarks rather than reading everything linearly. This is exactly the payoff of the semantic HTML5 chapter — a page built from generic <div>s gives a screen reader nothing to jump between; yours should let you skip straight to "Skills" or "Contact" without listening to everything before it.

View source, cold

Open your file's raw source (not the rendered page) and read it top to bottom as if you'd never seen it. Does the nesting make sense without the browser's rendering to lean on? If a section is buried three levels deeper than it should be, or a closing tag is in the wrong place and the browser "fixed" it silently, this is usually where you'll spot it.

Duplicate IDs

Search your file for every id="..." and confirm none of them repeat. Duplicate IDs are invalid HTML (the validator will catch this too), and they break both in-page anchor links and label/input associations in ways that can be genuinely confusing to debug, since the browser doesn't reliably tell you which duplicate it decided to use.
