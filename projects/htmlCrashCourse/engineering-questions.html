Engineering questions & Socratic vision

No single correct answer sits above these. They're meant to be argued with yourself, or a mentor, out loud — the goal is noticing the decisions you made without realizing you were making them.

  1. You wrapped your Skills section in <section> rather than <div>. What actual, concrete difference does that make — to a browser, to a screen reader, to a search engine — given that with no CSS applied, both would render visually identical? If the difference is invisible to you right now, does that mean it doesn't matter?
  2. You chose between <ul> and <dl> for your skills. Defend your choice specifically: what would be lost, or gained, if you'd made the other choice instead? Is there a skills-section design where neither is really correct?
  3. Your contact form has an action attribute pointing nowhere real. What is the actual, complete set of things that happen right now if someone fills it out and clicks Submit in a real browser? Walk through it literally, step by step, rather than assuming.
  4. You used <strong> once, deliberately, rather than wrapping your whole bio in it or using none at all. What would be lost if every sentence in your bio were wrapped in <strong>? Is there a version of "everything is emphasized" that's functionally the same as "nothing is emphasized" — and if so, why?
  5. You have exactly one <h1> and one <main>. What would actually go wrong — concretely, not just "it's against the rules" — if your page had two of each? Who or what would notice, and how?
  6. You wrote real alt text for your image instead of leaving it empty or writing "photo." Try to describe, in your own words, what information a blind user gains from a well-written alt versus a lazy one, using your specific image as the example. Now: is there ever a correct reason for alt="" (empty, not missing)? What kind of image would that be for?
  7. This entire project has zero CSS and zero JavaScript, on purpose. Look at your finished page and identify one thing about it that's genuinely better for that constraint, not just "acceptable despite it."
  8. If a stranger opened only your raw HTML source, with no rendering, could they tell what each section of your page is for, just from the tag names and nesting? If the answer is no anywhere, what does that tell you about where the "meaning" of that part of your page currently lives?
