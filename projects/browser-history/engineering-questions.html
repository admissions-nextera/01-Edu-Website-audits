Engineering questions & Socratic vision

These questions don't have a single correct answer sitting in a chapter above. They're meant to be argued with yourself (or a mentor) out loud. The goal isn't to recall what you built — it's to notice the design decisions you made without realizing you were making them, and to defend them.

  1. When Back() fails because the stack is empty, you chose a status code for that. Why that one, specifically? What would a client of your API have to do differently if you'd chosen 400 instead of 409, or vice versa? Is "no history to go back to" actually an error at all, or is it more like an empty search result — a valid outcome that just happens to not be what the user wanted?
  2. Your History struct holds one back stack, one forward stack, one current URL — for the entire server. What happens to two different users hitting your API at the same time? Is that a bug, given what the spec asked for? If it is a bug, what's the smallest change that would fix it, and what's the largest?
  3. You protected History's state with a single mutex covering every field. Could back and forward have been protected by two separate mutexes instead? Would that have been better, worse, or just different? What would have to be true about how the operations use the two stacks for splitting the lock to even be safe?
  4. Visiting a new page clears the entire forward stack, unconditionally. A browser really does this. Can you think of a plausible product reason a different app might not want this behavior — where "branch and keep the old forward history around somewhere" would actually be useful? What would that require changing about your data structure?
  5. Right now your history lives entirely in a Go process's memory and vanishes on restart. Suppose you were told: "this needs to survive a server restart." Walk through, out loud, everywhere in your code that assumption is currently baked in, before you touch a single line to fix it.
  6. Back and Forward are near-mirror images of each other. When two pieces of code are structurally that similar, is that a sign you should factor out a shared helper, or is it a coincidence of this particular problem that isn't worth abstracting? What would you actually lose or gain by merging them into one move(from, to *[]string) (string, bool) style function?
  7. You used a slice as the backing store for your stacks. What is the actual time complexity of your Push and Pop, including whatever append does under the hood? Under what usage pattern would that complexity ever matter for this particular project — and under what usage pattern would it clearly not?
  8. If a grader (or a future teammate) opened only handlers.go and never looked at history.go, could they tell what your API does, just from reading the handler bodies? If the answer is no, what does that tell you about where the "real" documentation of this service's behavior currently lives?
