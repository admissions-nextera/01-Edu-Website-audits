Overview: what we're building and why

Every browser you've ever used has a Back button and a Forward button. Under the hood, that behavior is almost always implemented with two stacks:

  • A back stack: pages you've visited before the current one, most recent on top.
  • A forward stack: pages you've gone "back" from, that you could go "forward" to again.
  • A current page: wherever you are right now.

The rules that make this feel right are exactly stack rules — last in, first out. The most recently visited page is the first one you land on when you hit Back. And the moment you visit a genuinely new page while sitting somewhere in your history, your "forward" options stop making sense and get thrown away — you can't go forward into a future that no longer exists once you've branched off into a new one.

This project asks you to implement exactly that logic, then put an HTTP face on it: a small server with endpoints for visiting a URL, going back, going forward, and checking where you currently are. That's the second half of last session — taking a plain Go type and making it reachable over HTTP, the same way a real backend service exposes its internal state and operations to the outside world.

Nothing here needs a database, a frontend, or a browser. You'll interact with your server using curl or a tool like Postman. The whole point is the two ideas underneath: correct stack behavior, and a clean HTTP layer sitting on top of it.
